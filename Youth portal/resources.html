01 · Your story on one page

How to write a CV

A CV is a focused snapshot of your experience, skills and potential. Make the most relevant evidence easy to spot.

01

Lead with the essentials

Put your name and professional contact details at the top. Add your city, a working phone number and an email address you check. A LinkedIn or portfolio link is useful if it is current.

02

Use a simple structure

Try: short profile, skills, education, relevant experience or projects, and references only if requested. Put the most relevant section first. Recent graduates can lead with education or projects.

03

Show evidence, not a task list

Start bullets with an action: built, organised, assisted, designed or analysed. Explain what you did, how you did it, and what changed. Add honest numbers where you can.

04

Tailor it to the role

Use relevant terms from the opportunity description when they genuinely match your experience. Give examples for important skills instead of copying a long list of keywords.

05

Keep it readable

Use clear headings, consistent dates, a legible font and enough white space. Avoid tables, text boxes and graphics that can confuse applicant-tracking systems. Save and reopen the final PDF to check it.

06

Protect your personal details

Do not include your ID number, banking details, passwords or full home address on a CV. In South Africa, state relevant qualifications clearly; never claim a certificate you have not earned.

Achievement formula: Action + task or context + result. Example: “Organised a weekly peer study group for 12 classmates, helping the team complete two group projects on schedule.”

02 · A focused introduction

How to write a cover letter

A strong letter connects your experience to this specific opportunity. It should add context to your CV, not repeat every line.

  1. Address the role.Name the position and organisation. Use a contact person’s name when it is publicly provided; otherwise, “Dear Hiring Team” is fine.
  2. Open with a reason.Say what you are applying for and give one specific reason the opportunity interests you.
  3. Prove your fit.Choose one or two relevant examples. Explain your action and the outcome, including school, volunteer or personal projects when relevant.
  4. Close clearly.Thank the reader, show interest in discussing your application, and sign off professionally. Keep the tone warm and direct.

A simple outline

[Your name and contact]
[Date]

Dear [Hiring Team / Name],

I am applying for [role] at [organisation]. I am interested because [specific reason].

Through [project / work / study], I developed [relevant skill]. For example, [action and result].

Thank you for considering my application. I would welcome the chance to discuss how I could contribute.

Kind regards,
[Your name]

Protect yourself while applying

  • Never pay to apply: Be cautious of requests for application, interview, uniform or “processing” fees.
  • Check the source: Look for the vacancy on the organisation’s official website and verify the sender’s email domain.
  • Pause at pressure: Urgent offers, guaranteed jobs and requests for banking details or one-time passwords are warning signs.
  • Share personal data carefully: Do not send your ID or financial documents to an unverified recruiter.
